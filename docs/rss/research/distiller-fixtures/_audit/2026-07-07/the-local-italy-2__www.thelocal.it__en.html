RANKED: Which Italian cities have the best climate?

Miranda Mullings - news@thelocal.it
Published: 7 Jul, 2026 CET. Updated: Tue 7 Jul 2026 17:04 CET
  • Add a comment

The Adriatic coast has the best climate in Italy, both in the short- and long-term, two recent climate livability studies have found.

Ancona is the city in Italy with the best climate, according to the 2026 Climate Index by Italian newspaper Corriere della Sera and iLMeteo.it, Italy's leading weather website.

The capital of the Marche region scored best of Italy’s provincial capitals, based on 17 indicators – varying from the humidity to the number of rainy days, but especially focusing on heat, from extreme temperatures to sustained heatwaves.

In 2025 Ancona never had a day with temperatures exceeding 33°C.

Following in second place is another Adriatic capital – Bari.

A separate climate index put together by financial newspaper Il Sole 24 Ore and weather site 3bmeteo put Bari, the capital of Italy’s southern Puglia region, in the top spot for the third year in a row. That report slotted Ancona in fourth place. 

The difference is due to the reports’ vastly different methodologies. The Sole 24 Ore and 3bmeteo report considers climate over a 10-year period – as opposed to a one-year snapshot of the climate in 2025.

Based on data collected on 15 indicators from 2015 to 2025, the Sole 24 Ore index found that the top four provincial capitals were on the Adriatic Sea, with Barletta-Andria-Trani (Puglia) and Pescara (Abruzzo) ranking second and third respectively.

While the reports differ on the exact ranking, they both found that the Adriatic coast is faring better than the rest of the country in terms of climate.

"The Adriatic coast has recently been in a better position compared to the Tyrrhenian coast from a climatic standpoint," said meteorologist Mattia Gussoni, who was part of the research team for the Corriere and iLMeteo.it report. 

"The Tyrrhenian basin, like the entire western Mediterranean, is warming more than the eastern region. The Adriatic coast is more affected by cooler air coming in from the Balkans."

The Sole 24 Ore report specifically noted the worsening climatic conditions in northern Italy, which the researchers noted is experiencing more extreme weather events, having fewer cold days, and is warming significantly faster than the rest of the country. 

“In some northern cities (Varese, Monza, Lodi, Turin, Cuneo, La Spezia), the moving average temperature has risen by more than 3°C since 2010,” the report stated.

The differences between the two reports become pronounced when comparing how Italy’s largest cities not on the Adriatic measure up in the short versus medium term.

Catania, Rome and Naples ranked significantly lower in 2025 (Corriere and iLMeteo’s index) than they did when considering the decade average (Sole 24 Ore and 3bmeteo’s report), suggesting that these places had worse-than-usual weather conditions last year.

On the other hand Palermo, Genoa and Turin enjoyed unusually mild weather last year compared to those cities' longer-term outlooks.

Milan, Florence and Bologna remain in the bottom rankings when it comes to climate, whether looking at it from a yearly or decade-long lens.

Here’s how the Sole 24 Ore report ranked Italy’s 10 largest cities (out of 107 provincial capitals):

1. Bari
24. Naples
31. Genoa
36. Catania
37. Rome
62. Palermo
71. Milan
72. Bologna
78. Florence
90. Turin

Here’s how the Corriere report ranked the same cities:

2. Bari
23. Genoa
41. Palermo
44. Naples
74. Milan
77. Turin
80. Bologna
89. Catania
91/92. Rome and Florence (tie)

Where in Italy has the worst climate?

As for the Italian cities with the worst climates, four of the five last-ranking according to Corriere’s 2025 data are in northern Italy.

The cities that rank the lowest according to Sole 24 Ore’s multi-year climate data vary significantly. The bottom five consist of two northern cities in addition to locations in central and southern Italy and in Sardinia.

While both reports considered indicators like susceptibility to extreme weather events and sustained heatwaves, their differing results show the extent to which looking at short-term versus long-term data affects findings.

Cities that ranked the lowest for climate livability in the Sole 24 Ore report:

  1. Asti (Piedmont)
  2. Caserta (Campania)
  3. Belluno (Veneto)
  4. Terni (Umbria)
  5. Southern Sardinia (Carbonia)

Cities ranked that ranked the lowest in the Corriere report:

  1. Oristano (Sardinia)
  2. Sondrio (Lombardy)
  3. Trento (Trentino–Alto Adige)
  4. Cremona (Lombardy)
  5. Mantova (Lombardy)
